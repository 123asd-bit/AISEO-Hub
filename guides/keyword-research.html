Keyword Research for Beginners

Keyword research helps you understand the language people use and the questions they need answered. Use metrics as evidence, not as the only decision factor.

Step 1: Define the audience

Write down who the page is for, what problem they have, and what a useful result looks like.

Step 2: Brainstorm seed topics

Start with a few broad subjects. Expand them into questions, comparisons, tutorials, and task-specific searches. Try our Keyword Ideas Generator for brainstorming.

Step 3: Validate with data

Use a keyword research tool for location-specific volume and competition estimates. Record the tool, database or geography, and collection date. Different providers may report different metrics.

Step 4: Inspect the search results

Review the top results and note content type, freshness, search features, and unanswered questions. Do not assume a keyword is easy solely because a tool reports a low difficulty score.

Step 5: Classify intent

  • Informational: learn how or why.
  • Commercial: compare solutions before deciding.
  • Transactional: complete an action.
  • Navigational: find a particular site or page.

Step 6: Map keywords to pages

Give each important page one primary query and a coherent set of related terms. Avoid creating several near-identical pages for the same intent. Use internal links to connect related content.

Step 7: Measure outcomes

After publishing, track impressions, clicks, queries, indexing, and conversions where relevant. Update content when the evidence or user needs change.

Record in your project report: keyword, intent, search volume, difficulty, source tool, geography, collection date, and planned target page.
